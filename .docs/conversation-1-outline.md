## I. Personal Introductions & Shared Challenges

### A. First Speaker (Dallas) — Non-traditional Student Perspective
- Returned to college ~5 years after high school; observes many students struggle with time management and adult responsibilities
- Started community college at age 30 (12 years out of high school), with kids and a full-time job
- Had to self-teach organizational and time management skills
- As a motivated older student, felt pressure to "catch up" on career progress
- Tendency to overcommit — says yes to too many opportunities (clubs, events, career-building activities, including this hackathon)
- Struggles with **personal knowledge management**:
  - Uses **Obsidian** (a note-taking/linking app) but still finds it insufficient
  - Core problem: if he doesn't take good notes, he loses context and can't pick up where he left off
  - Scheduling, tracking deadlines, and allocating enough work time remains difficult
  - Frequently scrambles at the last minute due to poor time allocation

### B. Second Speaker (Zia) — International / Career-Changer Perspective
- Lost his father to a heart attack at age 7; grew up wanting independence as quickly as possible
- Joined the police force at age 17
- Describes himself as a "curious soul" who wants to learn everything — which creates overload
- Interested in machine learning, data analysis, research, reading — but ends up paralyzed ("doing nothing")
- Repeatedly tries to create schedules/timetables but ends up tearing them up in frustration
- At work, time management was easier because the employer provided structure (schedule, plan, tasks)
- Outside of work (social life, family), time management remains a major challenge
- Strongly resonates with the time management problem as a project idea

### C. Third Speaker (Wellington) — Context-Switching & Skill-Building Perspective
- Echoes the same time management struggles
- Identifies **context switching** as a major additional challenge:
  - Goes deep into one topic, then when returning to another, loses track of where he was
  - Needs something to help organize and track skill-building across multiple domains
- Introduces the concept of a **"Skill Builder"**:
  - References a cybersecurity website that shows skill paths (e.g., "if you want to be a SOC analyst, here are the certifications and steps")
  - Works backwards from a goal to create a roadmap
  - His own career path was non-linear: Computer Science -> Computer Engineering (hardware) -> back again
  - Has broad "basketball skills" rather than one deep specialization
  - Frequently asked by students and family members for career guidance (e.g., "I want to do avionics — what should I do?")
  - Notes that **career guidance is largely absent** — people are just told "go to university" without a clear path

## II. Problem Synthesis & Convergence

### A. Identifying the Core Problems (Group Discussion)
The team identifies several distinct but related problems:
1. **Lack of clear career/life pathways** — no guidance on how to get from point A to point B
2. **Inability to measure productivity** toward chosen goals outside of work
3. **Difficulty managing multiple life domains** (career, family, health, education, social)
4. **Context switching costs** — losing progress when jumping between pursuits
5. **Cognitive overhead of planning** — building and maintaining schedules is itself time-consuming

### B. Dallas Expands on the Vision
- **Destination discovery**: You can't chart a path without knowing what destinations exist; exploring the space of possibilities is its own problem
- **Career guidance limitations at universities**:
  - Freshman advising appointments (~30-60 min/semester) are mostly spent on degree requirement planning
  - Little time is devoted to long-term career exploration or skill development
  - The future is changing rapidly (especially with AI), making static guidance insufficient
- **Life as a pie chart**: Envisions allocating time across life domains (kids, career, study, sleep, health, networking) with target hours per week
- **The planning trap**: Building a 2-week schedule takes significant time; if you fall behind on maintaining it, the whole system collapses
- **Research validation**: Dallas has conducted interviews with other students and found these problems are widely shared
- **Current state**: Most people fall back to the simplest free tools (notes apps, basic calendars) because nothing easy and comprehensive exists
- **The "personal assistant" analogy**: Wealthy executives have secretaries/life coaches/personal assistants — students don't have access to that

## III. The Project Idea: "Evolver" / AI-Powered Student Assistant

### A. Dallas Reveals Prior Work
- Has been developing a concept called **"Evolver"** — an AI-powered personal assistant for college students
- Entered into the **Oklahoma Entrepreneur's Cup**
- Sent executive summary to teammates prior to meeting
- Target users: college students (free for them)
- Value proposition for universities: better student performance benefits the institution
- No code written yet — only specification/design documents so far
- Acknowledges the full vision is complex and probably too large for the hackathon

### B. Proposed Feature Areas for the Hackathon
The team discusses potential features, understanding they need to scope down:

1. **Time Management & Scheduling**
   - Help students plan and track their time
   - Reduce cognitive overhead of schedule creation/maintenance

2. **Career Exploration & Guidance**
   - Explore career options and destinations
   - Chart skill-building paths backward from goals

3. **Skill Building / Tracking**
   - Track progress along learning paths
   - Manage context across multiple skill domains

4. **Academic Support**
   - Track homework assignments
   - Note-taking / knowledge management

5. **Multiplayer / Social Features** (stretch goal)
   - Coordinate with other users
   - Find shared availability for study sessions

6. **Integration with Existing Tools**
   - Rather than replacing tools students already use, integrate with them
   - Google Calendar, Outlook, and other apps with open APIs
   - Meet students where they are instead of forcing a tool switch
